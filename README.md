# I.	Abstract
Tuberculosis (TB), caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, remains a leading global cause of infectious disease mortality, with 1.25 million deaths reported in 2023, including 161,000 among people living with HIV (PLHIV) [1]. TB disproportionately affects vulnerable populations, and individuals with HIV face a 16-fold increased risk of developing active TB due to immunosuppression [1,4]. This study investigates the global burden and determinants of TB and TB-HIV coinfection by integrating epidemiologic and socioeconomic data from the World Health Organization (WHO) and World Bank across country-year observations from 2015 to 2023.
Exploratory data analysis revealed strong right-skewness across outcomes and significant geographic disparities, with the highest burden concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa. Machine learning regression models were developed using a time-based split to preserve temporal structure. Tree-based models achieved strong predictive performance, with test R values up to 0.95. Feature importance analysis demonstrated that TB burden is driven by socioeconomic conditions, healthcare access, and HIV prevalence, while TB-HIV outcomes are primarily influenced by HIV epidemiology. These findings highlight the need for integrated public health strategies addressing both structural determinants and HIV-related risk factors.
